Six jobs for the trainer ProiOS 27

Each job starts with one message in your own words. A change to your training comes back as a proposal card.

  • Build a complete programGive it your goal, days a week, session length and gym. It starts from the best fit of the 17 programs and swaps the lifts you avoid. Then it proposes the whole training block on weekdays: the workouts, the start day, 1 to 16 weeks, and a deload week when the program has one. For a rolling cycle, it writes the workouts itself. Review program lets you edit every workout before you tap Apply program.
  • Set your next targetsAfter you save a session, it reads every set and proposes each lift's next load and reps, with the reason on each line. The app can ask for you: it puts “Set my next targets for Lower A from the session I just saved (Thu 1 Oct).” in the chat. A squat at the top of its reps with RPE to spare can go up 10 to 20 lb; a lift under the top of its range usually gets more reps first. You approve or reject each line. The app never changes a load by itself.
  • Change a session while you liftBetween sets, ask for a lighter day, a swap when the rack is taken, an added lift or a lift taken out. It changes only the sets you have not logged. Ask for the change for today only, or for the workout too.
  • Change workouts and blocksSwap a lift in the program, or change its sets, reps, rest, warm-ups and target RPE. Move a missed session, add a deload week, change a block's dates or days, or make it a rolling cycle, such as 3 on and 1 off for 8 cycles. It renames, moves, copies and deletes workouts, and renames and deletes folders. It also changes your units, rest, bar and plates, and logs a body measurement.
  • Explain your trainingAsk why your bench stayed at 185, how the week went, which lifts are going up or where your records stand. It reads your sessions, records, weekly totals and sets per muscle, and it answers with your own numbers and dates.
  • Remember youIt keeps a rules file on you: goals, preferences, limits and a line per lift. It follows the file in every answer and writes its own note on a lift after a session. You can edit the rules file and read its lift notes, and you write lift notes of your own. When you change the file, the trainer follows your version.

Every recommendation names its research, for example (Schoenfeld 2017), and you can open the paper. With Claude, it can also search the web and list its sources. It answers how-to questions about the Block app from the app's help. It gives no medical advice.

Ask it as you ask a coach

Type in your own words. Each request below shows what comes back.

  • Build me a 4‑day program for a 500 lb deadlift in 16 weeks.

    It starts from a 4‑day strength program and sets it to 16 weeks with a deload week. Review program opens it, and Apply program saves the training block.

  • Set my next targets for Lower A.

    It reads the last Lower A session set by set. The card has one line per lift, with the change and its reason: Back Squat 3 × 8 at 185 → 3 × 8 at 195, after 3 good lifts at RPE 7. Approve saves a line; Approve all saves every line.

  • Why did my bench stay at 185?

    It names the set that missed and the lamp that went red, with the load and the reps. Nothing changes.

  • I'm about to lift Upper B. I slept 4 hours, so go lighter today.

    A card, Upper B: start now, lists every lift at today's loads. Start session opens it. The workout keeps its own numbers.

  • The leg press is taken. Swap it for today.

    In a session, it proposes a lift for the same sets, reps, rest and target RPE. Apply changes the session. The sets you logged stay as they are.

  • Replace skull crushers today and in the program.

    In a session: one card in two parts, The workout and Today's session. Review workout shows the change to the workout, and its Apply changes both.

  • I missed Wednesday. Where does Lower B go?

    In a block with training days, a card, Move Lower B, puts it on a free day left that week. Apply moves it.

  • Make Block 2 a rolling cycle: 3 on, 1 off, 8 cycles.

    A card, Change Block 2, shows the new rhythm. After Apply, each session is due from your last one, so a break does not use up the block.

  • How many sets did my chest get each week this month?

    It adds up your working sets per muscle, week by week, and answers with the numbers.

  • Switch me to kg. My gym has a 20 kg bar and plates down to 1.25.

    A card, Settings, lists the unit, the bar and the plates. After Apply, every load it proposes uses them.

Open the trainer ProiOS 27

The same chat opens from each place below. Each place offers its own first questions.

You need Block Pro and iOS 27. Without Pro, a trainer button opens the plans; a new account may see its days of Pro or free messages first. On iOS 26 and earlier, the trainer's buttons do not show, and Settings > AI trainer says Needs iOS 27.

  • On Today

    Tap Ask trainer, beside Start session at the bottom of Today. With no workouts yet, Today and Plan show Build with trainer first.

  • On the Plan tab

    Tap New block, then Build with trainer, or New workout, then With the trainer. About you, Programs and your assistants are in Settings.

  • In Progress or a session

    Tap the trainer button in the top row. It is a speech bubble with three dots. In a session, the trainer sees every set you logged so far.

  • On other screens

    A program's page has Adjust with the trainer. A finished block's page has Plan next block. The saved scoresheet can show Ask trainer with the next targets.

  • In Settings

    Tap the sliders at the top right of Today, Plan or Progress to open Settings. The AI group is under Block Pro, at the top. Tap AI trainer. This screen shows who answers on this iPhone and the messages left this month. It also has What you can do, Ask trainer, Your rules and Clear chat.

Build your first program ProiOS 27

Start with About you. The trainer and Programs plan from it.

  1. Fill in About you

    Open Settings and tap About you. Choose your Goal and your Training experience. Set Your week: the days and the minutes. Choose Where you train.

    Under Limits and likes, add the lifts you want and the lifts you avoid. Tap Save.

  2. Ask for the program

    With no workouts yet, tap Build with trainer on Today or the Plan tab. When Program setup has no goal, days, minutes or equipment, it opens first: fill it in and tap Save and build. The trainer adapts the program that fits best in one answer, and the app checks the plan before the card shows.

    Or type your own request, for example “Build me a 4‑day upper/lower program, 60 minutes, full gym.”

  3. Answer its questions

    For a request you type, it asks a short question when it does not know your goal or your days a week. For other facts that are missing, it uses a default, such as a full gym, and says what it assumed.

    With Claude through Block, Claude Sonnet 5 builds the program. Your answers to its questions stay with Sonnet 5 until the proposal comes.

  4. Read the card

    It starts from the best fit of the 17 programs. It swaps the lifts you avoid or cannot load. It can also drop lifts or move a workout to another day.

    Programs run on weekdays. For a rolling cycle, such as 3 on and 1 off, ask for one: the trainer then writes the workouts itself, or a block change makes your block rolling.

    The card has the program's name, for example Start Strength Upper Lower 4. It lists the start day, the weeks, the deload week, the training days and each workout. A program has no loads: choose them in your first session, and the next session starts at the loads you lifted.

  5. Tap Review program

    • Change the program's name, Starts, the weeks and the Deload week.
    • Pick the workout for each weekday, or Rest. A rolling block that the trainer wrote shows its days on and off and its cycles instead.
    • Tap a workout to change its lifts and sets.
    • Swipe left on a workout to remove it. Tap Add workout for a new one, or Reorder to drag them.

  6. Keep draft or Apply program

    Keep draft, at the top of the review, keeps your edits on the card in the chat. It saves nothing to your workouts.

    Apply program saves the training block. Its workouts show on the Plan tab, ready to start. To drop the proposal, tap Dismiss on the card.

Next targets after a session ProiOS 27

The app never changes a load or reps by itself. After every session, the trainer reads what you lifted and sets each lift's next load and reps, with the reason, and you approve or reject each lift. Until you approve it, the next session starts at the numbers you lifted with Block Pro, or at the workout's numbers without it.

  1. Finish and save

    Tap Finish in the session's top row. On the scoresheet, tap Save session. If some sets are not logged, the button reads Save completed sets.

  2. Let the trainer ask

    The saved scoresheet has a panel for the trainer's next targets. When the trainer can answer at once, the app sends this question in the chat for you: “Set my next targets for Lower A from the session I just saved (Thu 1 Oct).”

    It can answer at once when you saved your own Claude key or already allowed Claude. It can also answer at once where Apple's model answers.

    With Claude through Block, this question counts as one of your messages, and Claude Sonnet 5 answers it. Its lines then show on the scoresheet.

    Otherwise the panel says No new targets from the trainer. The next session starts at the numbers you lifted. Tap Ask trainer to open the chat with the same question. The card then comes in the chat, with Review.

  3. Read each line

    Each line shows one lift's change and its reason, for example Back Squat 3 × 8 at 185 → 3 × 8 at 195, after 3 good lifts at RPE 7.

    The trainer reads each set (the load, the reps, the RPE and the lamps), your notes on the lift, the rep range and the week of the block. Every set a good lift at the top of its range: it adds the lift's step, 10 lb or 5 kg for a big lower-body lift and 5 lb or 2.5 kg for any other, and the reps start again at the bottom. Under the top: more reps at the same load, or more load when your sets show you are ready. It leans toward progress and holds only for a reason it names, such as a hard session (an RPE over the target, a red lamp) or a deload, and a whole workout can hold. A note that names the next step comes first, once its condition is met.

    After a session of good lifts, it does not lower a load unless you ask. The app sends back a line that lowers a load after good lifts, or keeps a load after every set reached the top of its range, unless the line gives the reason, such as your note, a deload or a cable stack at its last plate.

  4. Approve or Reject each line

    Each line on the scoresheet has two buttons:

    • Approve saves that lift's new targets to the workout.
    • Reject leaves the lift as it is.
    • Approve all and Reject all decide every line you have not decided.

    A hold needs no decision. When every lift holds, the page says No changes. Every lift holds. Nothing changes until you approve. Lines you leave wait on Today with Review, and they never apply by themselves.

When the trainer has not answered

No connection, the month's messages used up, Claude not allowed yet or an error: Today shows No new targets from the trainer. Lifts start at your last numbers. Tap Ask trainer beside it to ask again. To keep a lift where it is, tell the trainer why, for example “The leg press sled ends at 900 lb.”

During a session ProiOS 27

In a session, the trainer reads every set you logged, with its lamps. It answers for the set in front of you first.

  1. Open the chat

    Tap the trainer button in the session's top row, beside Finish. The line under the message field shows In session and the workout's name.

  2. Ask about your next set

    Ask “What should I lift on my next set?” or “That set was too heavy. What now?” It answers in a sentence or two, from the sets you logged and your history.

  3. Ask for a change

    It can make four kinds of change:

    • The load or the reps of the sets you have not logged.
    • A lift swapped for another.
    • A lift added.
    • A lift taken out.

    A swap keeps the sets, reps, rest and target RPE. The old lift's loads go, unless the trainer gives new ones.

  4. Choose where the change goes

    • Today: the lamps judge today's numbers. The workout keeps its own.
    • The workout: Review workout shows the change, and Apply workout saves it from now on. This session stays as it is.
    • Both: one card with The workout and Today's session. Review workout shows the workout's change, and one Apply changes both.

    When your words do not say which, it asks you.

  5. Tap Apply

    The session changes at once. The trainer never changes a set you logged. A lift with a logged set is done for today: the trainer does not change it, in the session or in the workout. Its next targets come after you save the session. To fix a logged set, open it and change it yourself.

Or start the session from the chat

Before you lift, tell it “I'm about to lift Upper B, lighter today.” The card, Upper B: start now, lists every lift as you will lift it. Tap Start session to open the session.

To move the workout on first, say “I'm about to lift Upper B. Move it on first.” The trainer sets the next targets on their own card: approve the lines, then tap Start session.

To change the workout itself first, such as a swap from now on, the order or rest, the card is the workout's: Review workout, then Apply workout. That starts no session: tap Start session on Today.

Change your plan ProiOS 27

Ask for all your changes in one message. One request gets one card with one Apply, even across several workouts.

  • Change a workout

    Ask “In Upper A, swap barbell rows for chest-supported rows, 3 × 10–12.” It can change sets, reps, rep ranges, rest, warm-ups and target RPE. It can also make a stretch a prep lift, which is logged and not judged.

    A card for one workout shows Review workout. In the review, tap Apply workout. A card for several workouts shows Review workouts, and its review has one Apply for all of them. Applying a change to a workout never starts a session.

    At Apply, the app checks that the workout did not change after the proposal. If it did, the card tells you to ask the trainer again. A card for a block or a folder does the same check.

  • Move a missed session

    In a block with training days, tell it “I missed Wednesday.” The card, Move Lower B, moves the session to a free day left that week, or skips it this week.

    A rolling block needs no move. The next session is due at once.

  • Add a deload or change the block

    Ask “Make week 6 a deload” or “Start Block 2 next Monday.” The card is named for the block, for example Change Block 2. It can change the name, the start day, the length, the training days or the deload week.

    The app does not make a deload week lighter by itself. Today marks the week. Tell the trainer you are about to lift, and it sets the deload's loads, sets and reps on a card for that session only: the workout keeps its own numbers, and the session after the deload starts at them.

  • Make a block a rolling cycle

    Ask “Make Block 2 a rolling cycle: 3 on, 1 off, 8 cycles.” The workouts then run in their order, not on weekdays. Each session is due from your last one, so a break does not use up the block.

  • Organize workouts and folders

    Ask it to rename, move, copy or delete a workout, or to rename or delete a folder. It proposes a delete only when you ask for one. A deleted workout's sessions stay in Progress.

  • Change settings or log a measurement

    Ask “Set my default rest to 2:30” or “I weigh 181.4 today.” The Settings card changes units, default rest, bar and plates. The measurement card logs the numbers you give it for that day.

Your rules and lift notes ProiOS 27

The trainer keeps a rules file on you and a note on each lift. You can read and edit the rules file. You can read its lift notes and write your own.

  • Open Your rules

    Open Settings and tap AI trainer. Under What the trainer knows, tap Your rules. The trainer writes the file under four headings, Goals, Preferences, Limits and Lifts, with one point on each line.

  • Let the trainer write the first file

    After you save About you or import from Hevy, the trainer writes the file if you have none yet and it can answer at once. Otherwise, Your rules shows Ask the trainer to write it.

    It writes the file from About you and your history. With Claude through Block, writing the file counts as a trainer message.

  • Edit it

    Type your changes. Tap Save. The trainer reads the file in every answer and follows your version.

    ## Goals - 500 lb deadlift by March ## Preferences - Sessions under 70 minutes ## Lifts - Leg press: the sled ends at 900 lb

  • Tell it in the chat

    Say “No barbell back squats. Use the safety bar.” It rewrites the file at once, with no card, and says in a line what it saved.

    The first time it writes warm-ups for you, it saves its warm-up rule under Preferences. Change the rule there, and it follows your rule.

  • Read the lift notes

    In a session, tap NOTES under a lift's name. The notes open in parts:

    • Today: the lift's last result, No new targets when the trainer has not set new ones, and the workout's note.
    • Trainer: its note on the lift, such as a cue or what to watch. You can read it. To change it, tell the trainer.
    • Yours: your own note, such as seat height or grip. Type it in Single lift. It saves when you leave the field.

    The trainer reads your note when it is about the question.

Why it says what it says

Each recommendation names its reason and its source. You can open the paper behind it.

The trainer cites a research card in brackets after each point, for example (Schoenfeld 2017). The Block app has 37 research cards. Each card gives one finding in plain words and its evidence level, with each source's DOI.

Under the answer, Research lists each paper with its topic and evidence level. Tap a paper to open it at doi.org. Two cards, Warm-up sets and Soreness and pain, have no study behind them. They show as Coaching practice.

A proposal of new loads, a new workout, a block or a session with new numbers must name its research cards, or the app refuses it. When a point comes from somewhere else, the trainer says so: your sessions, your notes, your rules file or your own request. The trainer is told to cite only these cards, never a study from memory.

With Claude, the trainer can search the web for facts the app does not have, such as a competition's rules or a program you name. While it searches, a line reads Searching the web. Sources, under the answer, lists each page it cites.

For a question about how to use the Block app, it answers from the app's help. It names the buttons as the app shows them.

Limits and questions

What do I need?

Block Pro and iOS 27. Without Pro, a trainer button opens the plans; a new account may see its days of Pro or free messages first, and after its days of Pro the chat opens to read. On iOS 26 and earlier, the app shows Needs iOS 27 for the trainer, and no new targets come: the next session starts at the numbers you lifted. Change a workout in the workout editor. Block Pro is $4.99 a month, or $39.99 a year with a two-week free trial for new subscribers.

Does it give medical advice?

No. For soreness or a tweak, it can propose lighter loads or a lift that avoids the painful movement. For an injury, or pain that is sharp or that lasts, it tells you to stop that movement and see a doctor or a physical therapist. It keeps to your training and the app.

Does anything change without Apply?

Changes to your workouts, blocks, settings and About you wait for Apply, and next targets wait for Approve, line by line. Three things save at once, with no card: your rules file, its note on a lift, and feedback you ask it to send. The app itself never changes a load or reps.

Why did it stop with Try again?

An answer that runs past its length limit stops, and the chat shows none of its text: tap Try again, or ask for one part at a time. A card the trainer made before the stop stays, ready to apply.

What do Not answered and Chat status mean?

Not answered, under your question, means it was not delivered. The Chat status panel under it gives the next step: Try again or Edit question after a connection or model failure, Review permission when Claude is not allowed yet, Check access for a Pro problem, Check messages when this month's messages are used up. A retry sends the same question again. It does not post a copy.

Why does it say Chat paused?

Pro access or this month's messages stopped the chat. The monthly cost limit stops it the same way. Earlier messages stay readable. Check access asks the server about Pro again, and Check messages asks about the messages left. Neither sends your question. Send it again when access is back.

How do I clear the chat?

Tap Clear at the top of the chat, or Clear chat in Settings > AI trainer. It removes the chat from this iPhone. Changes you applied stay.

Where is the chat kept?

On this iPhone, for your account. It stays when you sign out. Another iPhone does not get it. Deleting your account deletes it.

Does it need a connection?

Yes. With no connection, nothing answers, and your question stays in the chat. Tap Try again when you are back online.

Can I tell the team something?

Yes. Tell the trainer what broke or what you want, in your own words. It sends it to the team, and the chat says Feedback sent.
