Strength, built in blocks.

Block is a lifting log for iPhone. Plan a block of weeks, then lift it one set at a time. With Block Pro, three lamps judge each set, and the AI trainer sets your next loads in the plates you own.

How a block runs

You plan the weeks once. After that, every session starts from the last one.

  1. Plan the block

    Group your workouts into a block: a start date, a number of weeks and an optional deload week. Set your training days. Today shows the session of the day.

  2. Lift the session

    Log each set as you lift it, in the plates you own. With Block Pro, three lamps judge the set while you rest.

  3. Get the next targets

    After the session, the AI trainer proposes each lift's next load and reps from what you lifted: more reps, more load or the same again. You approve or reject each lift. Until you approve one, it starts at the numbers you lifted with Block Pro, or at the workout's numbers without it.

Three lamps on every set Pro

Each set with a target gets three lamps. White is a pass. Red is a miss, and the reason shows under it.

  • RepsWhite when you did every rep the set asks for.
  • LoadWhite when the bar was at the planned load. For an assisted lift, at most the planned load.
  • EffortWhite at or under the lift's target RPE (8 unless you set one). No RPE counts as a pass.

Three white lamps make a good lift. One red lamp makes a no lift. A weight-and-reps set that beats the target's estimated max passes reps and load. Warm-ups and drop sets are not judged.

The lamps are part of Block Pro. Without Pro, you log the same sets with no lamps.

How the trainer decides

How the trainer sets each lift's next load and reps
1A good lift: all reps, full load, RPE at or under the target RPE.

Short answers

Which iPhones?

iPhones on iOS 17 and later. The AI trainer needs iOS 27.

What is a block?

A set number of weeks of the same workouts, with an optional deload week at the end. After each session, the AI trainer (iOS 27) sets the next loads. Blocks are part of Block Pro.

What is a good lift?

With Block Pro, a set with a target and three white lamps is a good lift. One red lamp is a no lift. Warm-ups and sets without a target are not judged.

Do I have to rate my effort?

No. A set without an RPE passes the effort lamp. An RPE over the lift's target (8 unless you set one) turns it red.

Is the import free?

Yes. The import, logging, workouts and history are free. Block Pro adds the lamps, blocks and the AI trainer.

Does the trainer give medical advice?

No. The AI trainer (Pro, iOS 27) keeps to your training and your log. For pain or an injury, it tells you to stop that movement and see a doctor or a physical therapist.

Where is my data?

In a database at Supabase, on Amazon Web Services. It is encrypted in transit and at rest, and no other account can read it. The app has no ads and no analytics SDKs. The privacy policy has the details.

Can I delete my account?

Yes. Today > Settings > Delete Account removes your account and all training data at once. It does not cancel a Block Pro subscription.
